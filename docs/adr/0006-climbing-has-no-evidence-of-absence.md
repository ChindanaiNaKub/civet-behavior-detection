# Climbing has no evidence of absence

No Climbing-labelled source clip was delivered, and [the footage audit](../FOOTAGE-AUDIT.md) found no unambiguous climb in a sampled-frame screen. That is the whole of the evidence. Treat Climbing as an open class that the delivered set happens not to label, not as a class the footage is known to lack. `MODEL_CLASSES` in [`../../scripts/labels.py`](../../scripts/labels.py) stays at three classes until a full interval pass either produces a Climbing interval or the annotation guide rules the candidate intervals out.

## Why

- The audit states its own limit: the screen sampled one frame every five seconds, so an 8-second clip gets one or two frames, and "this is a coverage check, not a full behavior annotation, so it cannot prove climbing is absent." Downstream docs then hardened that screen into a design constraint: the audit says "Climbing has no source clip" and "Do not present a four-class result", [ADR 0004](0004-train-a-cnn-over-window-frames.md) says "No verified climb exists in the supplied footage", and `labels.py` omits the class. An absent observation became an absent behaviour.
- Two artifacts share the premise that Climbing is absent, so neither can test it. The audit's absence claim and the pre-labeler smoke test's "every Climbing proposal is a false positive, 4 of 4" both assume the same premise, and the smoke test used the audit as its only check. This is the one premise the whole class list rests on, and nothing has tested it directly.
- The instrument omits the class. The Phase 1 checklist slide (slide 5 of `CMZ-Phase-1-Behavior-Detection.pptx` and of `CMZ-Phase-1-Field-Guide-Redesign.pptx`) lists Eating, Resting, Locomotion, Transition and Uncertain and has no Climbing row, while the Phase 2 brief names four classes. An annotator working from the presented checklist cannot produce a Climbing interval whatever the footage shows.
- The enclosures carry climbable structure and the animal uses it. Camera 03 holds an inclined log and a raised left ledge; Camera 02 holds a raised ledge behind the planter, a hanging rope and the planter itself. Reviewing frames found at least two intervals in the locked **test** split that a reviewer applying the guide as written has to decide: `Locomotion/25690518090944462.mp4` around 4.5-7 s, where the animal crosses the inclined log and goes up onto the raised left ledge, and `Locomotion/25690518091918610.mp4` around 11-16 s, where it leaves the floor and ascends the raised ledge behind the planter. Reproduce with `ffmpeg -ss 4.5 -t 4 -i data/raw/Locomotion/25690518090944462.mp4 -vf fps=2,scale=760:-1,tile=4x2 out.jpg`. Camera 02 ledges also appear in `Locomotion/25690518091711167.mp4`, `25690518091847591.mp4` and `25690518091948946.mp4`, and the animal rests against the planter in `Resting/25690520152850102.mp4`.
- Whether those intervals are Climbing is a definition call, not an observation, and the guide has not made it. The glossary says "movement up or down a sloped or vertical structure, or active climbing along it", and also "horizontal travel on a structure is Locomotion". A one-step ascent onto a ledge sits on neither side of that line clearly.

## Consequences

- Produce the interval annotations without this premise as an input. Do not tell an annotator that the footage has no climbing before they review it, and use `review.py` as it stands, which already offers Climbing because `labels.ALL_LABELS` includes it.
- Two intervals decide the class list. Review `25690518090944462` and `25690518091918610` first, at the timecodes above, and record either a Climbing interval or a written reason they are Locomotion. One sitting, minutes rather than hours.
- Then make the boundary a rule. If a step onto a ledge is Locomotion, say so in [`../ANNOTATION-GUIDE.md`](../ANNOTATION-GUIDE.md) and add the Climbing row to the checklist slide, so the exclusion is a documented definition and not an inference from an audit that disclaims it.
- If the class list stays at three, the report and the deck must say a Climbing-labelled clip was not supplied and say which intervals were reviewed and ruled out, rather than saying the footage has no climbing. Both candidate intervals sit in the locked test split, so a Climbing interval found there retires the current test set and the `MODEL_CLASSES` list, and `split.py` will refuse a split that leaves the class out.
- Scope question for the professor, and it is not answerable from the repository. The brief names four classes for Task 1 and describes four rooms and four cameras; the delivered set has three folders and two camera overlays. Ask whether Climbing is required and whether footage from the other rooms exists. This is the decision [ADR 0005](0005-split-by-clip-not-by-recording-group.md) and the [plan](../PHASE-2-PLAN.md) both list as a risk and neither can close.
